Hitting the Wall Boston 2014 · 31,655 finishers · 5 km splits

The wall is at 30 km

Pace across a whole marathon field, segment by segment. The median runner holds about 5:03 per km through 10 km and is past 6:00 per km by 35 km. Most of that change comes after 25 km.

  • +13%median pace at 25 to 30 km, compared with the settled 5 to 10 km pace. It was +6% one segment earlier and +19% one segment later.
  • 15%median late slowdown for an individual runner: their own 35 to 40 km pace against their own 10 to 20 km pace.
  • 27%of finishers slowed by more than 25% on that same comparison.

Where the field slows

Every finisher of the 2014 Boston Marathon crossed timing mats at 5, 10, 20, 25, 30, 35 and 40 km. Dividing each segment's time by its length gives a pace, and the median across 31,655 runners gives the shape above. It is nearly flat to 20 km and drifts through 25 km. Then it climbs sharply into 30 km and stays high. By 35 to 40 km the median is about a minute per kilometer slower than at the start.

Slower runners slow far more

Grouped by finish time and measured against each group's own early pace, runners under 3:00 stay close to flat. Runners finishing after 4:30 are 30% or more slower in the closing segments.

Runner by runner

The individual late-slowdown figure has a median of 15% and a long right tail, which is where the 27% who lost more than a quarter of their pace sit.

What this doesn't show

Split times locate the slowdown. They can't say whether glycogen, muscle damage, heat or a fast start caused it. Boston's Newton hills run from about 26 to 34 km, right on the corner, and the net-downhill opening makes early pace look quicker than it would on a flat course. This is one race on one day. The companion study, The Negative-Split Myth, uses the same field.

Method

Data
Official 2014 Boston Marathon results via the public llimllib/bostonmarathon mirror
Sample
31,655 finishers with every split present and strictly increasing
Pace
Segment time divided by segment distance, in min/km; field value is the median
Late slowdown
Each runner's 35 to 40 km pace against their own 10 to 20 km pace
Run it
python fetch_data.py then python analyze.py (Python, pandas, NumPy, matplotlib)
